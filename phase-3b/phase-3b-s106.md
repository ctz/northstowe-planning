**"Affordable Housing Dwellings"**

Dwellings to be provided as Affordable Housing in accordance with Schedule 2;

**"Affordable Private Rent"**

means those Dwellings that are within a Build to Rent scheme and let by the landlord (which need not be a Provider) at a rent that is at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable);

**"Affordable Private Rent Lettings Criteria"**

means approved criteria prepared by the Owner and approved by the District Council which the Owner shall apply in letting the Affordable Private Rent. As a minimum the criteria shall prioritise unless otherwise agreed with the District Council:

- a) Applicants with an identified level of income (such level to be set out in the criteria);
- b) Applicants with a local connection/support network. This would include parents, grandparents, siblings, children;
- c) Applicants with a local work connection, with an 'active travel map' to be provided to show the relevant radius from the Site;
- d) Ground floor units or units that are accessible for wheelchairs to be prioritised for wheelchair applicants;
- e) If at any time the criteria cannot be met due to lack of applicants then it would cascade out to anyone living in South Cambridgeshire District;

**"Affordable Rent"**

a rent that is set in accordance with the Affordable Rents Policy, Annex 11 of the Greater Cambridge Housing Strategy (as amended or substituted from time to time) or as otherwise defined in National Policy at the relevant time;

**"Affordable Rent Default Dwelling Mix"**

means:

- 1 bed units — 35%
- 2 bed units — 35% (approximately 50/50 houses/flats)
- 3 bed units — 25%
- 4 bed units — 5%

or such alternative mix of Dwellings (in terms of number of bedrooms, number of intended occupiers and property type) as shall be agreed or determined at Review as being the preferred mix for the Affordable Rented Dwellings for a Key Phase;

**"Affordable Rented Dwellings"**

Affordable Housing which meets all of the following conditions unless otherwise agreed in writing with the District Council:

- (a) the rent is set:
  - (i) in accordance with the Government's rent policy for Social Rent; or
  - (ii) at the lower of:
    - (A) Affordable Rent, or
    - (B) rent that is at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable) and not exceeding the Local Housing Allowance level for the area; or
  - (iii) for affordable housing for rent within a Build to Rent scheme at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable); or
  - (iv) at such alternative rent level as may be agreed in writing between the Owner and the District Council from time to time having regard to relevant and up to date National Policy at that point in time;
- (b) except in the case of Affordable Private Rent the landlord is either a Provider or is a community led group such as a charitable trust or community trust and is approved by the District Council in relation to its allocations and land management arrangements;
- (c) it includes provisions to remain at an affordable price for future eligible households, or for the subsidy to be recycled for alternative affordable housing provision;
- (d) all Affordable Rented Dwellings other than Affordable Private Rent to be let in accordance with the Local Lettings Plan and through its choice based lettings scheme, known as 'HomeLink', or its successor, unless approved by the District Council for non-registered providers and to the extent of any inconsistency between the two documents the Local Lettings Plan shall take precedence; and
- (e) all Affordable Private Rent to be let in accordance with the Affordable Private Rent Lettings Criteria;

**66. Informative 3 — Environment Agency / Conservation response**

Planned crossing points over water courses on the site should be designed so as to avoid fragmenting existing water vole habitat and so that new crossings don't become barriers to the movement and migration of wildlife.

As proposed, existing habitats and features of biodiversity interest present on the site should be retained where possible, protected during work phases, enhanced and incorporated into the wider landscape design. The landscape design should include how habitats on the site will link up with each other as well as to the wider countryside. This is in line with the National Planning Policy Framework, which requires planning decisions to minimise impacts on and provide net gains for biodiversity, including establishing coherent networks that are more resilient to current and future pressures.

**67. Informative 4 — Other Environmental Informatives and Advice**

Surface Water Drainage and Infiltration Sustainable Drainage Systems (SuDS):

All surface water from roofs shall be piped direct to an approved watercourse or surface water sewer using sealed downpipes. Open outlets should not be used. Only clean, uncontaminated surface water should be discharged to any soakaway, surface water system or watercourse.

The water environment is potentially vulnerable and there is an increased potential for pollution from inappropriately located and/or designed infiltration (SuDS). We consider any infiltration (SuDS) greater than 2.0 m below ground level to be a deep system and are generally not acceptable.

All infiltration SuDS require a minimum of 1.2 m clearance between the base of infiltration SuDS and peak seasonal groundwater levels. All need to meet the criteria in our Groundwater Protection: Principles and Practice (GP3) position statements G1 to G13 which can be found here: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/groundwater-protection.

In addition, they must not be constructed in ground affected by contamination and if the use of deep bore soakaways is proposed, we would wish to be re-consulted. The proposals will need to comply with our Groundwater protection position statements G1 and G9 to G13.

## Annex 2 — Transport Review Group

### The role of the group

1. The TRG is a consultative group the role of which is to facilitate co-ordination between partners over the delivery of the transport strategy for the Development (and Phase 3A if relevant).

2. It will be responsible for reviewing the planning, monitoring and managing of travel behaviours, trip rates and the transport impact of the Development (and Phase 3A if relevant) and recommending responses accordingly, with a preference towards promoting sustainable travel behaviours where appropriate.

3. The TRG will not have any decision making powers and all decisions regarding transport strategy for the Development will rest solely with the County Council and/or the District Council as the case may be taking account of recommendations and representations from TRG.

4. TRG will have the role of responding to improvements proposed to the highways network so that the agreed transport strategy can respond to changing circumstances. The group is a key forum for discussing appropriate changes to both the nature and the timing of various elements of the transport strategy subject always to the statutory roles performed by the District Council as local planning authority and the County Council as local highway authority.

5. A key element of this approach is to inform and recommend to key stakeholders the level of funding and investment to be committed and proportionate use of resources across each phase, to achieve the best outcomes to ensure appropriate and achievable. In carrying out its functions, and operating within its terms of reference, TRG will work collaboratively and in cooperation with other relevant stakeholders to enable efficient use of resources and to avoid where possible any abortive works or expenditure.

6. In addition, the group will also be a forum for discussing and seeking opportunities for additional sources of funding for travel plan initiatives (and other transport interventions) relating to the Site.

7. The membership of the group, in the first instance, are proposed to be the District Council and the County Council with the Owner (or its development manager).

8. Other stakeholders and interested parties may be invited to meetings depending on the agenda for example:

   8.1 Representatives from the Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority;

   8.2 National Highways;

   8.3 Local bus operators;

   8.4 Smart Journeys, and other Northstowe Travel Plan Coordinators;

   8.5 Network Rail, Train Operating Company or other train operator;

   8.6 Other travel plan coordinators in the local area;

   8.7 Town or Parish Councils; and

   8.8 the Greater Cambridgeshire Partnership.

### Frequency of meetings, agendas and reporting

9. Meetings will take place quarterly beginning within 3 months of Implementation (or within 3 months of the implementation of Phase 3A if sooner), but the members may from time to time agree a different frequency or ad hoc meetings.

10. Responsibility for preparing and distributing agendas and minutes (liaising with other members in terms of agenda items) will lie with the County Council.

11. Minutes are to be circulated and agreed within 2 weeks from the relevant meeting subject to members first agreeing matters regarding the publicity of relevant minutes. Any specific actions required between meetings will be addressed via email.

### Outcomes of the Primary School Education Review

3.1 The purpose of the Primary School Education Review is to determine through reviews of child yield evidence in relation to the Development and having regard to Current Data the following:

3.1.1 whether a Primary School is required as part of the Development;

3.1.2 if required, the programme for delivery of the Primary School (based on when such school is required arising from the demand for places from the Development and the Development trajectory) and in turn the Trigger Events for:

(i) the submission and approval of the Free Serviced Primary School Specification for the Primary School,

(ii) the payment of the Primary School Contribution and

(iii) the Primary School Site Land Transfer.

### Outcomes of the Secondary School Education Review

4.1 The purpose of the Secondary School Education Review is to determine through reviews of child yield evidence in relation to the Development and having regard to Current Data the following:

4.1.1 the level of Child Yield arising from the Development in terms of FE; and

4.1.2 where this is beyond 20% of 4 FE (when any capacity (beyond that initially predicted) arising from Phase 2 and Phase 3A has been taken into account), the amount of the Secondary School Further Contribution (if any).

### Review Process

5.1 The Owner shall serve the Notice of Intention to Commence on the County Council not less than 60 Working Days prior to its intended date for Commencement of the Development.

5.2 When the County Council intends to commence an Education Review it shall serve written notice on the Owner during the relevant Review Period for that Education Review accordingly.

5.3 Within 20 Working Days following receipt of such notice, the Owner shall provide the County Council with the Actual Mix for the Development and its updated trajectory for the Development.

5.4 The County Council shall utilise the Actual Mix and trajectory provided by the Owner in the relevant Education Review.

5.5 Within 20 Working Days of receipt of the Actual Mix the County Council shall undertake the relevant Education Review and in so doing undertake the following:

5.5.1 a review of the evidence as to Child Yield from the Development. This will be based on the application of the Detailed Multiplier to the Approved Mix and the General Multiplier to the Development at the time of the Education Review to the Development Balance (but excluding from account any Specialist Housing which is agreed by the County Council to be such that it is unlikely to contribute to Child Yield), producing an indicative Child Yield rounded to the nearest decimal point (the "Indicative Child Yield"); and

5.5.2 having established the Indicative Child Yield, the County Council shall interrogate the Current Data and have regard to representations from the Owner (the County Council having first provided the Owner with the Indicative Child Yield, the Current Data and its working and given the Owner a reasonable period of time of not less than 10 Working Days to make such representations) and in so doing taking into account the trajectory and any capacity arising from Phase 2 and Phase 3A. As a result of such interrogation and taking into account any such representations from the Owner, the County Council shall determine its proposed outcome of the Education Review (the "Indicative Outcome") and shall notify the Owner of its Indicative Outcome and detailed workings and justification thereof;

5.5.3 Following notification to the Owner of the Indicative Outcome the Owner shall have 10 Working Days to make representations to the County Council in respect of the Indicative Outcome. Within 10 Working Days following receipt of any representations in accordance with the above the County Council shall having first had regard to any such representations write to the Owner setting out the Agreed Outcomes of the Education Review.
