## 1. Definitions and Interpretation

**1.1** The following words and phrases (including for the avoidance of doubt those contained in the Recitals) shall unless the context otherwise requires bear the following meanings:

**"1990 Act"**

means the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended);

**"Acts"**

means Section 106 of the 1990 Act, Section 111 of the Local Government Act 1972 and Section 1 of the Localism Act 2011 together with in each case any variation, substitution or statutory amendment, re-enactment thereof and all other statutory powers and acts pursuant to which the parties hereto shall be empowered to enter into this Deed;

**"Affordable Housing"**

housing which meets an identified housing need in the District Council's administrative area and is defined as affordable housing by National Policy subject to such criteria (including as to eligibility, discount, future affordability and recycling or refund of subsidy or grant funding) as may be set out for the relevant tenure in National Policy;

**"Affordable Housing Dwellings"**

Dwellings to be provided as Affordable Housing in accordance with Schedule 2;

**"Affordable Private Rent"**

means those Dwellings that are within a Build to Rent scheme and let by the landlord (which need not be a Provider) at a rent that is at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable);

**"Affordable Private Rent Lettings Criteria"**

means criteria prepared by the Owner and approved by the District Council which the Owner shall apply in letting the Affordable Private Rent. As a minimum the criteria shall prioritise unless otherwise agreed with the District Council:

a) Applicants with an identified level of income (such level to be set out in the criteria);

b) Applicants with a local connection/support network. This would include parents, grandparents, siblings, children;

c) Applicants with a local work connection, with an 'active travel map' to be provided to show the relevant radius from the Site;

d) Ground floor units or units that are accessible for wheelchairs to be prioritised for wheelchair applicants;

e) If at any time the criteria cannot be met due to lack of applicants then it would cascade out to anyone living in South Cambridgeshire District;

**"Affordable Rent"**

a rent that is set in accordance with the Affordable Rents Policy, Annex 11 of the Greater Cambridge Housing Strategy (as amended or substituted from time to time) or as otherwise defined in National Policy at the relevant time;

**"Affordable Rent Default Dwelling Mix"**

means:

1 bed units — 35%
2 bed units — 35% (approximately 50/50 houses/flats)
3 bed units — 25%
4 bed units — 5%

or such alternative mix of Affordable Rented Dwellings (in terms of number of bedrooms, number of intended occupiers and property type) as shall be agreed or determined at Review as being the preferred mix for the Affordable Rented Dwellings for a Key Phase;

**"Affordable Rented Dwellings"**

Affordable Housing which meets all of the following conditions unless otherwise agreed in writing with the District Council:

(a) the rent is set:
(i) in accordance with the Government's rent policy for Social Rent; or
(ii) at the lower of:
(A) Affordable Rent, or
(B) rent that is at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable) and not exceeding the Local Housing Allowance level for the area; or
(iii) for affordable housing for rent within a Build to Rent scheme at least 20% (twenty per cent) below local market rents (including service charges where applicable); or
(iv) at such alternative rent level as may be agreed in writing between the Owner and the District Council from time to time having regard to relevant and up to date National Policy at that point in time;

(b) except in the case of Affordable Private Rent the landlord is either a Provider or is a community led group such as a charitable trust or community land trust and is approved by the District Council in relation to its allocations and management arrangements;

(c) it includes provisions to remain at an affordable price for future eligible households, or for the subsidy to be recycled for alternative affordable housing provision;

(d) all Affordable Rented Dwellings other than Affordable Private Rent to be let in accordance with the Local Lettings Plan and through its choice based lettings scheme, known as 'HomeLink', or its successor, unless approved by the District Council for non-registered providers and to the extent of any inconstancy between the two documents the Local Lettings Plan shall take precedence; and

(e) all Affordable Private Rent to be let in accordance with the Affordable Private Rent Lettings Criteria;

### Informatives

### Informative 1 — Noise

To satisfy the noise insulation scheme condition for the residential building envelope and traffic noise, the applicant / developer must ensure that the residential units are acoustically protected by a noise insulation scheme, to ensure the internal noise level within the habitable rooms, and especially bedrooms comply with British Standard 8233:2014 "Sound Insulation and noise reduction for buildings — Code of Practice" derived from the World Health Organisation Guidelines for Community Noise: 2000.

The code recommends that a scheme of sound insulation should provide internal design noise levels of 30 LAeq (Good) and 40 LAeq (Reasonable) for living rooms and 30 LAeq (Good) and 35 LAeq (Reasonable) for bedrooms. Where sound insulation requirements preclude the opening of windows for rapid ventilation and thermal comfort / summer cooling, acoustically treated mechanical ventilation may also need to be considered within the context of this internal design noise criteria. Compliance with Building Regulations Approved Document F 2006: Ventilation will also need consideration.

### Informative 2 — Dewatering

Any small-scale dewatering in the course of building or engineering works which is greater than 20 cubic metres per day and does not meet the conditions of the groundwater abstraction exemption under Regulation 5 of the Water Abstraction and Impounding (Exemptions) Regulations 2017 will require an abstraction licence from the Environment Agency. The Environment Agency assesses applications to abstract water against local water availability. In groundwater bodies where water is already fully committed, there is a presumption against issuing new consumptive groundwater licences. In the case of dewatering we consider a licence to be consumptive where the water cannot be returned locally to the aquifer. Whilst this may be deemed acceptable for short-term dewatering where water is returned to the environment, this would be assessed on a case-by-case basis. However, in such cases a consumptive groundwater licence may not be issued long-term and the applicant must ensure that any construction is engineered such that permanent dewatering will not be required. This is especially important if the development is proposing sub surface structures such as basements.

### Informative 3 — Environment Agency Conservation response

Planned crossing points over water courses on the site should be designed so as to avoid fragmenting existing water vole habitat and so that new crossings don't become barriers to the movement and migration of wildlife.

As proposed, existing habitats and features of biodiversity interest present on the site should be retained where possible, protected during work phases, enhanced and incorporated into the wider landscape design. The landscape design should include how habitats on the site will link up with each other as well as to the wider countryside. This is in line with the National Planning Policy Framework, which requires planning decisions to minimise impacts on and provide net gains for biodiversity, including establishing coherent networks that are more resilient to current and future pressures. Where possible, new habitats should ideally be created and allowed to become established prior to removal of existing habitats. For planting, native species should be used, ideally of local provenance.

Further ecological surveys may be required in the future as development of the site progresses to ensure decisions are based upon contemporary information. Future ecological monitoring of the site following construction will record changes over time and demonstrate net biodiversity gain.

Adopting appropriate biosecurity measures ('Check, Clean, Dry') on site is encouraged as they can help to reduce the risk of inadvertently spreading invasive non-native species (INNS).

### Informative 4 — Other Environmental Informatives

**Surface Water Drainage and Infiltration Sustainable Drainage Systems (SuDS):**

All surface water from roofs shall be piped direct to an approved surface water system using sealed downpipes. Open gullies should not be used.

Only clean, uncontaminated surface water should be discharged to any soakaway, watercourse or surface water sewer.

The water environment is potentially vulnerable and there is an increased potential for pollution from inappropriately located and/or designed infiltration (SuDS). We consider any infiltration (SuDS) greater than 2.0 m below ground level to be a deep system and are generally not acceptable. All infiltration SuDS require a minimum of 1.2 m clearance between the base of infiltration SuDS and peak seasonal groundwater levels. All need to meet the criteria in our Groundwater Protection: Principles and Practice (GP3) position statements G1 to G13 which can be found here: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/groundwater-protection.

In addition, they must not be constructed in ground affected by contamination and if the use of deep bore soakaways is proposed, we would wish to be re-consulted. The proposals will need to comply with our Groundwater protection position statements G1 and G9 to G13.

### Informative 5 — Pollution Control

Surface water from roads and impermeable vehicle parking areas shall be discharged via trapped gullies. Prior to being discharged into any watercourse, surface water sewer or soakaway system, all surface water drainage from lorry parks and/or impermeable parking areas for fifty car park spaces or more and hard-standings should be passed through an oil interceptor designed compatible with the site being drained. Roof water shall not pass through the interceptor.

Site operators should ensure that there is no possibility of contaminated water entering and polluting surface or underground waters.

### Informative 6 — General Informatives

Notwithstanding the provision of the Town and Country Planning General Permitted Development Order 1995 (or any order revoking or re-enacting that Order), any oil storage tank shall be sited on an impervious base and surrounded by oil tight bunded walls with a capacity of 110% of the storage tank, to enclose all filling, drawing and overflow pipes. The installation must comply with Control of Pollution Regulations 2001, and Control of Pollution (Oil Storage) Regulations 2001.

Site operators should ensure that there is no possibility of contaminated water entering and polluting surface or underground waters.

## Annex 2 — Transport Review Group

### The role of the group

**1.** The TRG is a consultative group the role of which is to facilitate co-ordination between partners over the delivery of the transport strategy for the Development.

**2.** It will be responsible for reviewing the planning, monitoring and managing of travel behaviours, trip rates and the transport impact of the Development and recommending responses accordingly, with a preference towards promoting sustainable travel behaviours where appropriate.

**3.** The TRG will not have any decision making powers and all decisions regarding transport strategy for the Development will rest solely with the County Council and/or the District Council as the case may be taking account of recommendations and representations from TRG.

**4.** TRG will have the role of responding to improvements proposed to the highways network so that the agreed transport strategy can respond to changing circumstances. The group is a key forum for discussing appropriate changes to both the nature and the timing of various elements of the transport strategy subject always to the statutory roles performed by the District Council as local planning authority and the County Council as local highway authority.

**5.** A key element of this approach is to inform and recommend to key stakeholders the level of funding and investment to be committed across each phase, to ensure the best outcomes and proportionate use of resources to achieve appropriate and achievable use of resources.

**6.** In carrying out its functions, and operating within its terms of reference, TRG will work collaboratively and in cooperation with other relevant stakeholders to enable efficient use of resources and to avoid where possible any abortive works or expenditure. In addition, the group will also be a forum for discussing and seeking opportunities for additional sources of funding for travel plan interventions (and other transport initiatives) relating to the Site.

### Membership

**7.** The membership of the group, in the first instance, are proposed to be the District Council and the County Council with the Owner (or its development manager).

**8.** Other stakeholders and interested parties may be invited to meetings depending on the agenda for example:

**8.1** Representatives from the Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority;

**8.2** National Highways;

**8.3** Local bus operators;

**8.4** Smart Journeys, and other Northstowe Travel Plan Coordinators;

**8.5** Network Rail, Train Operating Company or other train operator;

**8.6** Other travel plan coordinators in the local area;

**8.7** Town or Parish Councils; and

**8.8** the Greater Cambridgeshire Partnership.

### Frequency of meetings, agendas and reporting

**9.** Meetings will take place quarterly beginning within 3 months of Implementation, but the members may from time to time agree a different frequency or ad hoc meetings.

**10.** Responsibility for preparing and distributing agendas and minutes (liaising with other members in terms of agenda items) will lie with the County Council.

**11.** Minutes are to be circulated and agreed within 2 weeks from the relevant meeting subject to members first agreeing matters regarding the publicity of relevant minutes. Any specific actions required between meetings will be addressed via email.

### Terms of reference

**12.** The terms of reference of TRG are (subject always to paragraph 3 above):

**12.1** to discuss:

**12.1.1** whether any highway or transport mitigation identified in this Agreement may be either pushed back to a later trigger or declared as not required and whether specified measures should be delivered in a different order (subject to no increase in cost to the Owner); and

## Annex 5 — Management Principles for Open Space

### Part 1 — Management Principles applicable where the Owner retains responsibility for management/maintenance

The principles to be followed in relation to Relevant Items where the Owner retains responsibility (for a defined period of time pending transfer to a Third Party or in perpetuity) are as follows (but without prejudice to the terms on which any assets are to be under the regime of a separate body e.g. Schools, adopted highways etc, which will be governed by the terms on which those assets are disposed).

**1.1** responsibility will remain with the Owner until the date of transfer to any Third Party where relevant;

**1.2** the Owner must obtain the written Approval of the District Council to the specification for the proposed maintenance regime for each Relevant Item to be maintained as it comes forward. The detail submitted must show what works are to be undertaken and the periodicity proposed for the same with the estimated costs of such maintenance covering a reasonable period of at least 10 years as is Approved by the District Council;

**1.3** provided that the service charge is a fair and proportionate charge based on the level of services which are being provided the Owner shall be entitled to levy an annual service charge on each Dwelling and an equivalent form of charge on Non-Residential Units but must secure the approval of the District Council of:

- **1.3.1** the frequency of payment; and
- **1.3.2** details of the mechanisms that will be used to ring fence funds secured by the charge so that such funds are used solely for the long term management and maintenance of the relevant Relevant Item(s) as appropriate to the circumstances;

**1.4** the District Council shall be deemed to have Approved the approach to service charges set out in 1.6 below;

**1.5** if the Owner wishes to propose an alternative funding regime this must be Agreed with the District Council (or Determined);

**1.6** unless otherwise agreed in writing by the District Council the service charge will be:

- **1.6.1** fixed at an initial sum appropriate in the market at the time and which is sufficient to provide income to cover the reasonable management and maintenance costs of to an agreed standard with allowance for a reserve or sinking fund;
- **1.6.2** fixed for an initial period from Implementation and then reviewed annually by reference to RPI and other material and relevant considerations;
- **1.6.3** rebased to the market every 10 years (or at such alternative time or interval as may be agreed with the District Council) taking into account the reasonable management and maintenance costs from Implementation to an agreed standard with allowance for a reserve or sinking fund; and
- **1.6.4** not increased by more than any increase in actual costs save with appropriate justification (and subject to being able to allow for a fair and reasonable level of reserve or sinking fund).

**1.7** a review as to the management and maintenance specification approved under 1.2 above may be carried out from time to time at the Owner's discretion and a report submitted to the District Council following any such review. Any proposals to amend the specification previously approved under 1.2 above shall first be Agreed in writing with the District Council;

**1.8** the Owner shall be responsible for putting in place all appropriate public liability insurance for the Relevant Item and for ensuring it is renewed annually;

**1.9** the Owner will ensure that the relevant arrangements can be adapted to changes in legislation and policy and agreed by the Owner in connection with facilities which might be offered on any Open Space or in relation to public access;

**1.10** the Owner will agree with the District Council the approach and principles of a forum to be established to actively facilitate local community engagement and participation in how Relevant Items can be used, managed and maintained by and for the benefit of the local community, such forum to comprise elected local community representatives and representation from the District Council and Northstowe Town Council;

**1.11** where the Owner proposes to charge for the use of any Relevant Item or part thereof such charges shall be reasonable and shall be reviewed on an annual basis or such alternative period of time as is reasonable in the circumstances.

## Annex 6 — Education Review

**1. Defined Terms**

- **1.1** "Actual Mix" the Approved Development Mix plus the Development Balance;

- **1.2** "Approved Development Mix" the most recently approved mix of Dwellings (including dwelling size, type and tenure) for that part of the Development that shall have received Reserved Matters Approval or otherwise been identified as part of the Permission at the time of the Education Review;

- **1.3** "Child Yield" the estimated number of children eligible to receive primary school education or secondary school education (as relevant) generated (and/or to be generated) by the Development determined in accordance with an Education Review;

- **1.4** "Current Data" data relevant to the matters to which the Education Review is directed available to the County Council and shared with the Owner which provides information to identify child yield and school capacity arising from previous phases of Northstowe; the number of children residing on the Development, or otherwise within a school catchment in the vicinity of the Development and also those attending a school or schools within the catchment in the vicinity of the Development and such sources may include (but are not limited to) any school monitoring information provided to the County Council by the Owner, NHS data, admissions data on the applications for primary school places and secondary school places within the catchment in the vicinity of the Development and Pupil Level Annual School Census Data (PLASC) and further including current information provided by the Owner on development trajectories and forecasts including in relation to tenures, mix and types of development intended for the rest of the Development and/or stipulated as part of the Permission;

- **1.5** "Detailed Multiplier" the County Council's approved detailed multiplier figures for assessing child yield arising from the Development applicable at the date of the relevant Education Review in relation to primary or secondary education (as appropriate) approved in accordance with the relevant policy approved pursuant to the County Council's constitution;

- **1.6** "Development Balance" the number of Dwellings for that part of the Development that at the time of the relevant Education Review has not received Reserved Matters Approval or otherwise been identified as part of the Permission;

- **1.7** "General Multiplier" the County Council's approved general multiplier figures for assessing child yield arising from the Development applicable at the date of the relevant Education Review in relation to primary or secondary education (as appropriate) approved in accordance with the relevant policy approved pursuant to the County Council's constitution;

- **1.9** "Specialist Housing" Dwellings which are of a type which is not likely to be occupied by children (for example specialist senior living or student accommodation).

**2. Working Principles**

- **2.1** The purpose of each Education Review is to determine, through reviews of child yield evidence in relation to the Development and having regard to Current Data, certain matters provided for in this Deed.

- **2.2** Under this Deed, the Owner is to provide:
  - **2.2.1** land and a financial contribution for two 3FE primary schools; and
  - **2.2.2** a financial contribution for a secondary school expansion comprising 80% of 4 FE plus a top up contribution based on child yield and the parameters set out in this Deed.

- **2.3** The County Council shall share all information reasonably required by the Owner to understand the approach being taken by the County Council to the Education Review and the decision made by the County Council.

- **2.4** For the purposes of each Education Review, Specialist Housing is deemed to not generate any Child Yield and shall be treated as such in the Education Review.

**Timing of Education Reviews**

- **2.1** The County Council shall commence the Primary School 2 Education Review no earlier than the Occupation of 1,200 Dwellings and no later than the Occupation of 1,250 Dwellings.

- **2.2** The County Council may commence the Secondary School Education Review no earlier than the Occupation of 1,800 Dwellings and no later than the Occupation of 3,000 Dwellings.

- **2.3** For the purposes of this Schedule, in respect of each Education Review, the Review Period shall be construed as the period of time between the earlier and later trigger point for the relevant Education Review as set out in paragraphs 2.1 — 2.2 above.

### Outcomes of the Primary School 2 Education Review

- **3.1** The purpose of the Primary School 2 Education Review is to determine through reviews of child yield evidence in relation to the Development and having regard to Current Data the following:
  - **3.1.1** the programme for delivery of Primary School 2 (based on when such school is required arising from the Development and demand for places from the Development trajectory) and in turn the Trigger Events for:
    - (i) the submission and approval of the Free Serviced Primary School Specification for the Second Primary School, and
    - (ii) the Primary School 2 Site Land Transfer

    PROVIDED THAT each such Trigger Event shall not be before the Occupation of the 1,300th Dwelling.

### Outcomes of the Secondary School Education Review

- **4.1** The purpose of the Secondary School Education Review is to determine through reviews of child yield evidence in relation to the Development and having regard to Current Data the following:
  - **4.1.1** the level of Child Yield arising from the Development in terms of FE; and
  - **4.1.2** where this is beyond 80% of 4 FE (when any capacity (beyond that initially predicted) arising from Phase 2 has been taken into account), the amount of the Secondary School Further Contribution (if any).

**5. Education Review Process**

- **5.1** If the County Council intends to commence an Education Review it shall serve written notice on the Owner during the relevant Review Period for that Education Review accordingly.

- **5.2** Within 20 Working Days following receipt of such notice, the Owner shall provide the County Council with the Actual Mix for the Development and its updated trajectory for the Development.

- **5.3** The County Council shall utilise the Actual Mix and trajectory provided by the Owner in the relevant Education Review.

- **5.4** Within 20 Working Days of receipt of the Actual Mix the County Council shall undertake the relevant Education Review and in so doing undertake the following:
  - **5.4.1** a review of the evidence as to Child Yield from the Development. This will be based on the application of the Detailed Multiplier to the Approved Development Mix and the General Multiplier to the Development Balance at the time of the Education Review (but excluding from account any Specialist Housing which is agreed by the County Council to be such that it is unlikely to contribute to Child Yield), producing an indicative Child Yield rounded to the nearest decimal point (the "Indicative Child Yield"); and
  - **5.4.2** having established the Indicative Child Yield, the County Council shall interrogate the Current Data and have regard to representations from the Owner (the County Council having first provided the Owner with the Indicative Child Yield, the Current Data and its working and given the Owner a reasonable period of time of not less than 10 Working Days to make such representations) and in so doing taking into account the trajectory and any capacity arising from Phase 2. As a result of such interrogation and taking into account any such representations from the Owner, the County Council shall determine its proposed outcome of the Education Review (the "Indicative Outcome") and shall notify the Owner of its Indicative Outcome and detailed workings and justification thereof.
  - **5.4.3** Following notification to the Owner of the Indicative Outcome the Owner shall have 10 Working Days to make representations to the County Council in respect of the Indicative Outcome.
  - **5.4.4** Within 10 Working Days following receipt of any representations in accordance with the above the County Council shall having first had regard to any such representations write to the Owner setting out the Agreed Outcomes of the Education Review.
